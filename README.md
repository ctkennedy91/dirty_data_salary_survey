# dirty_data_salary_survey
A real world dataset about salary surveys starting in 2021 where 6 out of 17 variables were free-form text entries.

In the process of trying to find a new job, I decided that it might be a good idea to work on my data cleaning skills before they shrivel up and die from lack of use. So, to Google I went in search of dirty data. I stumbled across a fun one. I found an article by Oscar Baruffa called [A real-world, messy dataset to practice on](https://oscarbaruffa.com/messy/) that contained a link to a Google Sheets salary survey spreadsheet. I personally didn't use Google Sheets. I downloads the dataset to Excel to clean it there. 

Baruffa mentioned that free-form text entries would result in a lot of cleaning to be done. Oh my goodness...there's some, umm, *interesting* responses when skimming through this.

But! I got the practice I was looking for! As I went through cleaning this dataset, I kept a change log. I may have went overboard with what I put in my change log. 

I kept an original copy of the dataset and cleaned it in a new copy. Within that new copy, I had two different sheets. One sheet removed most annual salary inputs of $0, which included students. The other sheet I left the student population in case the 'stakeholder' would want that information as part of the analysis.
